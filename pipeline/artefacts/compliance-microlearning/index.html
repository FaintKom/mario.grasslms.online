Section 1 of 6

The brief and the design stance

Performance gap Social efficiency + learner-centered Audit evidence Scope boundary

Performance gap. ISO 27001 cl.7.3 and ISO 27002 §6.3 require every person working under the organisation's control to be aware of the security policy and the consequences of nonconformity; GDPR Art.32(4) requires processing on instruction only; NIS2 Art.20(2) obliges management to provide cyber-hygiene training. Employees meet phishing, unlabelled data, unlocked screens and unreported incidents on an ordinary workday. Policy PDFs do not move those behaviours.

Ideology. Social efficiency: success is defined by behaviours the organisation needs and can evidence for an audit trail. Underneath it runs a learner-centred streak, since every unit anchors in the learner's own inbox, desk and Monday. Awareness training that ignores lived context buys compliant clicks.

Learners. Everyone: office, remote and frontline, all levels of digital fluency, zero assumed security vocabulary, time-poor. Prior knowledge is uneven, so the design keeps a low entry floor and puts personal relevance in the first twenty seconds.

Named scope boundary. Training can build recognition, correct micro-decisions, a reporting reflex and shared vocabulary. It cannot replace technical controls or repair a culture that punishes reporting. Those were flagged to the control owner as environment preconditions rather than papered over with content.

Five observable outcomes, each written with audience, behaviour, condition and degree, and each tagged with its Bloom level and knowledge type. The degree column is what makes the assessment criterion-referenced later.

Metacognitive thread. Every unit closes with a ten-second self-check: "where would this have caught me this week?" Connecting an outcome to the learner's own week transfers better than a knowledge check on its own.

Load rules applied. One objective per unit; worked example before independent practice; cue labels rendered on the artefact rather than beside it, so attention never splits; visual scenes with short text in place of narration over identical text; the whole course fits commute-sized sessions.

Section 5 of 6

Assessment and alignment

Criterion-referenced, never ranked. Unit 6 poses five fresh integrated scenarios. Pass is ≥4/5 and the critical item correct: reporting a suspected personal-data breach immediately even though the learner caused it. One hidden breach defeats the whole GDPR and NIS2 clock, so that behaviour cannot be compensated by knowing password rules. A fail routes to the owning unit with one variant re-attempt.

Three assessment modes. Diagnostic: the U1 cold probe, ungraded, surfaces overconfidence. Formative: every unit is decision practice with unlimited retries, and feedback explains the cue behind the verdict. Summative: Unit 6 only.

OutcomeTaught byAssessed byBloom match

Evaluation loop (Kirkpatrick). L1: two questions at exit. L2: the criterion pass, with the critical-item pass rate reported separately. L3: phishing-simulation click rate, report rate, and time-to-report on real incidents. L4: incidents caught early as a share of all incidents. Level 3 is the one the redesign listens to.

Section 6 of 6

What is built · Unit 1, playable

Unit 1 runs in a custom lesson shell with a step rail, progress, resumable local state and gates: the next step unlocks only when the current activity reports completion, meaning watch to the end, 7 of 9 on the check, a clean sweep in the simulator.

  • Film (~2 min). An animated cold open, assembled scene by scene.
  • Longread. The four tells, written for a busy reader, around 400 words.
  • Check your eye. Nine question cards, each answer explaining the cue behind it.
  • Inbox simulator. Three messages to open and flag. False alarms count against the sweep, so guessing does not pass.
  • Commit. One habit the learner names before leaving.
