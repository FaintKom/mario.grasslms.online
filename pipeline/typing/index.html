What this is

The scenario takes place inside a work messenger, which you already use every day. It needs no tutorial, and the writing can carry the whole scene.

Each of the four decisions can be made from a different rung of the ladder. The game never tells you that a choice was wrong. At the end it shows the sentence that was on your screen next to the sentence you replaced it with.

Where the framework comes from

The rungs come from the Ladder of Inference (observable data, selected data, meaning, assumption, conclusion, belief and action). Chris Argyris developed it at Harvard, and Peter Senge later brought it to a much wider audience.

The ladder is Argyris's; what I did was make it playable.

How Noor's feedback is written

  • Notes arrive during play and never as a report at the end.
  • They talk about how you decided and never about who you are.
  • Instead of grading a moment, Noor offers another way to read it.
  • The tone stays tentative, so that next time you need the note less.
  • There is no score, percentage or star anywhere.
  • Every note ends with one thing to try tomorrow.

How it was made

The whole scenario is one HTML file with no framework and no build step. Claude wrote most of the code while I argued with it about the details.

The longest part of the work was tuning how long the typing indicator stays up before each message. That pause is what makes the channel feel like a room with people in it.
